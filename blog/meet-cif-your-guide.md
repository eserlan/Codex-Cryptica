# Meet Cif, Your Little Guide to Codex Cryptica

_Lost in a menu? Wondering what a button does? There's a small hooded fellow who can help._

![Cif, looking curious](https://assets.codexcryptica.com/images/blog/meet-cif/cif-curious.svg)

Codex Cryptica has grown quite a bit. There are maps and graphs, generators, timelines, tables, a virtual tabletop, and enough settings to keep a particularly organised wizard occupied for an afternoon.

Which is great, until you find yourself thinking: _Hang on. Where was that thing again?_

That's where Cif comes in.

Cif is the little hooded fellow who lives in Codex Cryptica and helps you find your way around. Think of him as a guide who knows the building, rather than another tool you need to learn.

## Ask him the question you were about to Google

You don't have to know the name of a feature to ask about it. Try something like:

- "How do I turn on the hex grid?"
- "Where can I find my entity tables?"
- "How do I use the generators?"
- "What does this part of the map do?"

Cif can explain how a feature works, give you a few steps to follow, and link to the relevant help pages when you want more detail.

He also knows which part of Codex Cryptica you're currently using. A question about a grid makes rather more sense when you're already looking at a map, after all. And when you first open his chat, he'll offer a few questions suited to that part of the app. Handy if you're not sure where to start.

![Cif, thinking through a question](https://assets.codexcryptica.com/images/blog/meet-cif/cif-thinking.svg)

## Sometimes it's easier to show than tell

This is my favourite bit.

Sometimes an answer like "open the map controls, then find the grid settings" isn't terribly helpful. You've already been looking for the map controls. That's why you're asking.

So Cif can offer to **show you**. When the relevant control is available, you'll see a **Show me** button. Accept it, and he can take you to the right screen, open the appropriate panel, or highlight the control you're looking for.

He won't quietly start clicking through your work on his own. You choose whether to follow the suggestion, and his guidance doesn't change anything in your vault.

![Cif, explaining where to look](https://assets.codexcryptica.com/images/blog/meet-cif/cif-explaining.svg)

## A little help, right where you need it

Cif isn't tucked away in a separate documentation site. He's part of the app.

You can open him from the **Ask Cif** button and carry on asking questions as you go. On desktop, you can even pop his conversation into a separate window — useful if you're working on a map and would rather keep the instructions alongside it.

There's also an **Ask about this** shortcut in some parts of the interface. And if you don't feel like typing, those suggested questions are a perfectly good starting point.

Optional getting-started journeys and contextual tips can help you discover a few useful corners of the app as you go. Tips are opt-in, and you can dismiss them. The idea is to lend a hand, not interrupt your work.

## Cif and the Oracle have different jobs

One distinction worth making: Cif isn't the Lore Oracle wearing a little cloak.

The **Oracle** helps with your fictional world — its characters, places, stories and lore. **Cif** helps with Codex Cryptica itself — where things are, how they work, and how to get from "I want to do this" to the right button.

That separation is intentional. You should be able to ask how to do something without turning it into a conversation about your campaign. Cif uses limited information about the app screen you're on to tailor his guidance; he doesn't need to read your world's lore to tell you where the grid settings are.

![Cif, rather pleased to have helped](https://assets.codexcryptica.com/images/blog/meet-cif/cif-delighted.svg)

## Say hello

Next time you're in Codex Cryptica and something isn't quite where you expected, look for **Ask Cif**. Try asking him how to do something — particularly something you've had to go looking for before.

And if he can't help, I'd genuinely like to hear what you asked. That's probably the most useful way to make him better.

Small fellow. Big library. He knows his way around.
