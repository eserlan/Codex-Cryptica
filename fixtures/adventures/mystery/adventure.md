# The Silent Bell of Hallowmere

<!-- source-id: mys-overview -->

A non-linear mystery for a small party, playable in about 40 turns and usable with any rules. In the port town of Hallowmere, the great harbour bell has rung at midnight for three nights, yet the bell tower was found locked from the inside with no one in it and no dust disturbed. The party must work out who or what rang the bell, in whatever order the clues lead them, and decide what happens to the bell when the midnight tide comes in.

## Scene 1: The Harbour Market

<!-- source-id: mys-scene-1-market -->

> **Read aloud:** Gulls wheel over a crowded fish market. Stalls sag under crates of silver fish, and everyone seems to be glancing at the harbour bell tower on the cliff above the water, which stands bare and silent in the morning sun.

Rill Fenwick, the harbourside clerk, meets the party here and asks them to find out who rang the bell before the harbour conservancy seals the tower and blames the ferrymen. The party may begin the investigation anywhere: the market gossip, the tidemill across the harbour, or the chapel on the hill.

- **Scene condition:** If the party asks about the bell here, Rill says the bells was struck at midnight on three nights running and points them toward the tidemill.

## Scene 2: The Old Tidemill

<!-- source-id: mys-scene-2-tidemill -->

> **Read aloud:** A low stone mill leans over the harbour water. Its great paddle wheel turns slowly on the rising tide, and through an open hatch comes the smell of fish oil, mildew and rope dressing.

The wheel chamber holds fresh salt spray on every surface, though the tower is a quarter mile away. One clue: the bellows of the bell frame is well oiled with fish oil, and a length of rope hangs freshly cut above a man's head height. A second clue: the clerk's ledger, copied out in a careful hand, records which boats were in the harbour at midnight, and the bell time matches the moment the tide gate was opened. A third clue: salt water fills the bellows, though the bellows are supposed to blow air. The tidemill keeper, Keeper Dain, insists he closed the tide gate before midnight.

> **GM only:** A tidal bellows drives the harbour bell through a rope frame, so the bell rings whenever the tide gate opens, with no one at the tower.

- **Scene condition:** If the party examines the rope, they notice it was cut from above at head height rather than worn through.

## Scene 3: The Chapel of Saint Vey

<!-- source-id: mys-scene-3-chapel -->

> **Read aloud:** A small chapel of grey stone stands among graves above the harbour. Its door is open, and inside, a single candle burns beside a copper bowl of salt water.

Sister Osmund, the chapel keeper, shows the party the sexton's empty bed upstairs: the sexton, old Fenwick, died five winters ago, yet she swears to the party that she heard his tread on the tower stair at midnight. The copper bowl beside the candle is full of clean salt water drawn from the harbour, and a fourth clue: drops of the same salt water mark a trail of small wet footprints between the chapel and the tidemill, too small for a grown man. The crypt below the chapel is sealed with a warded door.

> **GM only:** Sister Osmund sends a youthful apprentice to fetch the salt water, and the small footprints are his, pacing the tide gate schedule.

- **Scene condition:** If the party searches the chapel, they find the sexton's old coat hanging on a nail, still smelling of fish oil.

## Optional Branch: The Tide Archive

<!-- source-id: mys-branch-archive -->

> **Read aloud:** Below the harbour office, a barrel-vaulted archive hums with dripping water. Shelves of tide tables stretch into the dark, and a clerk's desk holds a single brass key.

This side path can be skipped with no effect on the ending. If the party visits, the archive yields a copy of the tide schedule showing the bellows were installed to warn ships at the harbour bar, and a hint toward the crypt door. The desk's brass key fits neither lock in the chapel nor the tower.

> **GM only:** The archive copy of the tide schedule proves the bell frame was raised by the conservancy itself, and never by a hand.

## Scene 4: The Drowned Crypt

<!-- source-id: mys-scene-4-crypt -->

> **Read aloud:** A narrow stair drops into a flooded crypt. Black water covers the floor stones, and a warded door of lead bars the far wall, humming faintly.

The warded door guards the original bell frame mechanism. The party must get through it. There are two ways past.

- **By force:** The ward is strong and the lead is soft; a determined party can batter the door down in minutes, but the noise carries to the market.
- **By talk:** If a character names the bellows and the tide gate to the ward, or shows the archive tide schedule, the ward sighs open, because the conservancy raised the ward to protect the conservancy's own frame.

A fifth clue lies under the water: a carved tally on the crypt wall counts three strokes of the bell, and the tally marks are cut with the same chisel as the tide tables in the archive.

> **GM only:** The crypt tally was cut by the apprentices of the conservancy to mark the bell's test strokes, one for each night.

- **Scene condition:** If the party breaks the ward by force, the bell rings once, wildly, and every ferryman in the harbour looks up at the tower.

## Cast

<!-- source-id: mys-cast -->

The people the party can meet.

### Rill Fenwick

<!-- source-id: mys-npc-rill -->

Rill is a harbourside clerk with ink-stained fingers, eager to please. **Motivation:** Rill wants the harbour conservancy to stop blaming the ferrymen and will help anyone who clears them.

### Sister Osmund

<!-- source-id: mys-npc-sister -->

Sister Osmund is the chapel keeper, prim and devout, with a voice like a closing door. **Motivation:** Sister Osmund wants the bell's midnight ringing explained before the chapel's repute suffers.

### Keeper Dain

<!-- source-id: mys-npc-keeper -->

Keeper Dain is the tidemill keeper, grizzled and suspicious of strangers. **Motivation:** Keeper Dain wants the tide gate left alone and the bell mystery forgotten.

## Ending

<!-- source-id: mys-ending -->

The adventure ends when the party leaves the crypt for the last time. Two outcomes are possible, depending on what they prove.

### Ending: The Bell Rings True

<!-- source-id: mys-ending-bell -->

> **Read aloud:** The bell rings out over the harbour, true and clear, and every ship answers with its own horn.

The party proves the tidal bellows rang the bell, the conservancy is named as the frame's owner, and the harbourside crowd accepts the truth. Rill's clerks write it into the ledger in a careful hand.

### Ending: The Bell Is Silenced

<!-- source-id: mys-ending-silenced -->

> **Read aloud:** The tower falls silent. The harbour holds its breath, and the tide comes in under a dark sky.

The party leaves without proof, or the bellows are cut and the frame lies broken. The conservancy seals the tower, the ferrymen are blamed, and the midnight ringing is forgotten.
