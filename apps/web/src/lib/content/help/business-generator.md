## Business Generator

The **Business Generator** creates one specific organisation the party can visit, work for, compete with, investigate, protect or own. It answers: what does this business do, who runs it, who depends on it, what pressures is it under, and how does it create play?

### What you get

Every business has these parts:

- **Identity**: name, type, a short description, its reputation and what makes it distinctive.
- **What it does**: what it provides, who pays, why it is here, its main income, how it earns and whether it owns what it handles, how big it is, and how it is doing right now.
- **Leadership and key staff**: the owner (or a manager, see below), a few staff with roles and traits.
- **Clientele**: typical, valuable and undesirable customers, and anyone who is turned away.
- **Dependencies**: what it needs to keep going, and what happens if one fails.
- **Relationships**: rivals, suppliers, patrons, regulators and other parties, each with a reason it matters.
- **Pressure, asset, secret, opportunity and complication**: the trouble it is in and the chances it has.
- **Adventure hooks**: two or three hooks built from the business's own people and problems.

### Choosing a business type

Pick a genre first. The business types on offer change with the genre, so a cyberpunk business is a ripper clinic or a fixer front, not a blacksmith. You can also type your own.

### Players own this business

Turn on **Players own this business** if the party bought, inherited or founded it. The leader becomes a manager the party employs, and you also get starting strengths, liabilities, upgrade paths, recurring obligations and what success attracts. Obligations are named (payroll, rent, guild dues) and never priced.

### Keeping names straight

Later steps are told who the business, its owner and its staff are, so they should not rename the business or bring in unexplained people. If a name still turns up without an introduction, the page lists it so you can check it before you save. A rival or other party is only described by a graph link that fits it (a rival "competes with" the business, a supplier "supplies" it).

### Refining one part

Use **Refine one part** to rewrite a single section, such as the current pressure or a staff member. Everything else stays as it is. If you rename a person, the new name replaces the old one in the hooks and the other sections. With AI on, you can add a short steer, such as "make it about the harbour".

### AI and offline use

With AI on, the business is written in three steps, and each step builds on the last. If a step fails, only that part is written from the built-in tables, and the page lists which parts those are. With AI Disabled, every business and every rewrite is made from the tables.

### Saving to your vault

In your vault, saving creates:

- a **Faction** for the business, holding its full text;
- a **Character** for the owner or manager and each key staff member you leave ticked;
- links from the business to each of them (_owned by_ for the owner, _employs_ for staff).

Rivals and other parties that already exist in your vault are linked to it. Any party that is not there yet is offered as a new entry, which you can untick. Nothing you already have is changed, except for the new links. If you opened the generator from a Location, Character or Faction, you can also link the business to it.

On the public page, saving hands the business and its people to the app. Rivals stay in the business's text there, and you can link them later.
