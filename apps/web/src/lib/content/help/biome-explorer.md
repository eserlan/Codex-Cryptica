## What Biomes is

**Biomes** is a reference library of real-world environments, such as temperate woodland, boreal forest, grassland, hot desert, tundra, wetland, tropical rainforest, coastline, savanna and alpine mountains. Use it to picture a setting and to check that your version of it hangs together.

Each biome answers two questions: what does it look like, and why does it look that way and what can live there.

## Finding it

Open the navigation drawer and choose **Biomes**. The Library works without a vault open and without AI, and its text works offline.

## Browsing and searching

- Type in the search box to find biomes by name or by what they contain, such as "moss" or "monsoon".
- Use the **Filters** buttons, such as Cold or Dry, to narrow the list. Choosing several filters shows only biomes that match all of them.
- Open a card to see a larger picture and details on climate, landscape, plants, wildlife, resources and hazards. Each section opens and closes on its own.

## How to read the pictures

Most pictures are **AI illustrations** made by the Codex Cryptica team and checked by a person against the biome's description. They are labelled "AI illustration", and the tool and date are shown beside them. Where a good photograph under an open licence exists, it appears as well, with the photographer, licence and original source.

An illustration is a good guide to the mood and the typical plants and landforms, but it is not a photograph, and details can occasionally be wrong. Treat the written description and its sources as the reference.

## Limits to keep in mind

- This is a simplified reference, not a scientific classification or a climate simulator. Real places mix and overlap.
- Conditions are given as ranges, and each statement is marked as a **common pattern**, a **simplification** or an **exception**.
- Wetland and coastline describe a kind of place rather than a climate, so some climate rows do not apply to them.

## Making your own biome

Open a biome and choose **Make my own**, or choose **Start from blank** in the Library. You then see two columns for climate, terrain, plants, water, pressures and notes:

- **Reference** on the left shows the real-world values. It never changes.
- **My version** on the right is yours. Leave a box empty to keep the reference. A "Changed from reference" note appears next to anything you edit.

Give the biome a name, then choose **Save biome**. A name is required.

## Saving, reopening and sharing

Saved biomes are stored in your vault as notes, so they sync and back up with everything else. You will find them under **My biomes**, where you can open one, make a copy to try a variation, copy it as Markdown, or delete it.

- **Edit biomes in the Biomes tool.** If you change the note text by hand, Biomes replaces it with a fresh version the next time you save.
- **Copy as Markdown** and **Download Markdown** give you a readable document with the reference, your changes and the sources under separate headings. If your browser blocks copying, the text is shown so you can copy it yourself.
- A saved biome follows your vault's normal sharing rules. Reference links you add are never included when a vault is shared or published.
- You need an open vault that you can edit to save. Without one you can still browse, make a draft and copy it as Markdown.

## Comparing biomes

Choose **Compare** on any biome card, or **Add to compare** on a biome's page, to pick up to three biomes. The **Compare** tab then lines them up attribute by attribute: temperature, seasons, rainfall, height above sea level, water, landscape, plants, wildlife, resources and hazards. Where an attribute doesn't apply to a biome, such as temperature for a wetland, it says "Doesn't apply" instead of leaving a blank. On a phone, the same information is stacked by attribute so you don't have to scroll sideways.

## Fictional changes

If part of your environment isn't true to the real world, such as ash rain or a desert kept green by a magic spring, add it as a **fictional change** instead of editing the real-world boxes. Each change has:

- what is different,
- why it happens (we remind you if this is empty, but you can still save), and
- what follows from it.

Fictional changes are kept apart from the reference and from your other edits, both in the editor and in the Markdown you copy, so the real and the invented never blur together.

### The optional AI helper

If AI is switched on, **Suggest effects** offers up to three knock-on effects for a fictional change. It sends only that change and a short summary of your biome to the AI service you have set up. Nothing is added until you choose **Add** on a suggestion, and anything you add is yours to edit. With AI off, everything else works the same and the button is replaced by a note.

## Illustration prompt, your picture and your links

In the editor, the **Illustration prompt** box gives you a ready-made description that starts with "fantasy, illustration of" and includes your own wording and any fictional changes. Choose **Copy prompt** and paste it into the image tool you like. Biomes does not make pictures for you. **Keep prompt with this biome** stores it with the biome so it appears in the Markdown too.

Once a biome is saved you can **add a picture** of your own. It is stored in your vault with the biome and is shown separately from the library's pictures. You can also add up to ten web links (they must start with https://) to references you want to keep. Links stay private: they are left out when a vault is shared or published.
